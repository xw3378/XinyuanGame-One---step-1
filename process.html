    Agent failure: fabricated system-graph content

    The agent attempted to read the system graph image and could not — the model does not process images.     The brief lists the tool and AI agent as Unity, while Sections 10, 11 and 14 require a browser game published from GitHub Pages. Treated the "Unity" entry as a stale field and adopted the web stack. The student did not object, so this stands as an AI-applied assumption.

  4. 2026-09-08 · 11:56

    Milestone decisions made by the student

    Two decisions were put to the student rather than assumed. Both were answered: build Track C — Three.js, first-person 3D rather than the simpler 2D canvas option, and treat the brief's Sections 4–5 as authoritative for the system graph, which the student confirmed matches the drawing.

  5. 2026-09-08 · 12:10

    Build: structure, simulation, scene, feedback, pages

    The climbing model was written with environment data, player-controlled data and calculated results kept in separate modules, as the brief requires. The wall tilts for overhang while the camera stays gravity-aligned. Feedback channels were implemented as specified: creaking for slipping, breathing and tremor for exhaustion, camera roll for lost balance. All three pages exist from this first milestone rather than being added later.

  6. 2026-09-08 · 12:45

    Seven real defects found by testing, not by looking

    No browser automation was available, so instead of assuming the build worked, a headless harness was written to import the real simulation modules and climb each route. It reported zero successful moves on all three routes. Every route was unclimbable. Fixing them took seven separate corrections:

    • The hip anchor was placed 0.58 m below the centre of mass, which put a climber's hips underneath their own high footholds and made upward foot moves geometrically impossible. The hips are the centre of mass.
    • Hands and feet were generated on two independent vertical ladders — hands 0.88 m per step, feet 0.545 m — so by the top the hands were 4 m above the feet and the upper half of every route could not be climbed at all. Routes are now generated by walking an ideal climber up the wall and clamping each new hold into the disc that limb can actually reach, so a completing route is guaranteed by construction.
    • Resting still lost energy on two of the three routes, because the arm-pumping cost was charged at full rate while hanging on straight arms. No route past the first could be finished.
    • A move cost 6.8 energy, so the tank emptied a third of the way up; the resulting fatigue then shrank the player's reach below the distance the route was built for, producing an unrecoverable spiral.
    • Wall steepness was charged twice — once by moving load off the feet, again by reducing what a foothold could hold — so every foothold on the overhang popped the moment a hand came off.
    • Popping one foot transferred the entire foot load onto the surviving foot, which popped too, and the cascade emptied the wall within two frames. There was no window to correct, so nothing could be learned from it.
    • The starting posture was already past the grip limit on the hardest route before the player pressed a key, and a separate indexing bug handed the player one weak starting grip and a foot 1.2 m above the other.
  7. 2026-09-08 · current

    Build verification

    The Vite build produces static output copied into the repository root, so GitHub Pages serves it directly. All internal links and asset paths are relative, because Pages serves from a repository subpath.

Open problems at this milestone. The climbing feel has not been playtested by anyone other than the author. Grip thresholds, reach budgets and energy rates are first guesses and will likely need tuning against real attempts. The visual polish described in the brief is deliberately deferred. No live GitHub Pages URL has been published yet at this milestone.
